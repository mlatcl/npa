Probability is the structure on which the queueing models sit. We start from a pictorial definition of joint, marginal, and conditional probability, then the rules, then expectations, then the Poisson distribution as a distribution defined by a function rather than a table.

Probability Review

[edit]

Notation: we often write \(P(x,y)\) for \(P(X=x,Y=y)\). Unlike a generic bivariate function, \(P(x,y)=P(y,x)\).

The product rule relates joint and conditional. The sum rule recovers a marginal by summing out the variable you do not care about. Continuous analogues replace sums by integrals.

Bayes is not a third axiom; it is the product rule rearranged. The barrels example below is a small discrete inversion of the conditioning.

Figure: Two barrels: Barrel One holds 20 apples and 4 oranges; Barrel Two holds 4 apples and 8 oranges. A barrel is chosen uniformly at random and a single fruit is drawn.

Work the numbers on the board if time allows. The same pattern applies to coins, medical tests, or any two-hypothesis discrete update.

Independence

[edit]

We will often assume that two or more variables are independent. That means knowledge of one does not change the distribution of the other, \[ P(x)=P(x\mid y), \] so the joint factorises, \[ P(x,y)=P(x)P(y). \] Factorisation is what makes so much of the later analysis tractable: superposition of Poisson processes, Kleinrock’s independence assumption, and the product-form results for networks of queues all start here.

Expectations

A probabilistic model summarises our beliefs about a system. To say more we often summarise the model by its expectations.

The expected value of a function \(f(x)\) under a distribution \(P(x)\) is \[ \left\langle f(x)\right\rangle_{P(x)}=\sum_{x}P(x)\,f(x). \] You will also see \(E\{f(x)\}\) for the same quantity.

A Small Discrete Example

Suppose \(X\) takes values \(0,1,2\) and \(f(x)=x^2\).

\(x\) 0 1 2
\(P(x)\) 0.2 0.5 0.3
\(f(x)=x^2\) 0 1 4

Then \[ \langle x^2\rangle = 0\times 0.2 + 1\times 0.5 + 4\times 0.3 = 1.7, \] and \[ \langle x\rangle = 0\times 0.2 + 1\times 0.5 + 2\times 0.3 = 1.1. \] The expectation of \(x\) is the first moment, or mean: the centre of probability mass. The expectation of \(x^2\) is the second moment.

Variance

Variance is the second moment minus the square of the mean, \[ \sigma^2 = \langle x^2\rangle - \langle x\rangle^2. \] It is always non-negative. Its square root is the standard deviation. For the table above, \[ \sigma^2 = 1.7 - (1.1)^2 = 0.49, \qquad \sigma = 0.7. \]

Continuous Variables

So far \(x\) and \(y\) have been discrete. For a continuous quantity — the height of a computer-science student — we could discretise into bins, as the pictorial definition already did. That is awkward if we want to answer any question about height. A probability density function (PDF) does not give probabilities directly; we derive them from it by integration.

Figure: A Gaussian PDF representing heights of computer scientists.

We write a density with a small \(p(\cdot)\) and a discrete distribution with a capital \(P(\cdot)\). A density is a positive function whose integral over the region of interest is one. The Gaussian above is a model of height. The probability that a student is taller than \(2\,\mathrm{m}\) is the integral from 2 to \(\infty\). Because the total area is one and the function is positive, that probability lies in \((0,1)\). The probability of being exactly \(2\,\mathrm{m}\) is zero: that is the limit of a region whose width tends to zero.

Manipulating Densities

The same rules apply. We take as given \[ p(y\mid x)=\frac{p(x\mid y)\,p(y)}{p(x)}, \] with \(p(x,y)=p(x\mid y)p(y)\) and \(p(x)=\int p(x,y)\,dy\). Discrete and continuous variables may be mixed. Expectations under a density use an integral, \[ \langle f(x)\rangle_{p(x)}=\int f(x)\,p(x)\,dx. \]

Cumulative Distribution Functions

A common question is \(P(x<y)\). The function that stores the answer for every \(y\) is the cumulative distribution function (CDF), \[ P(x<y)=\int_{-\infty}^{y} p(x)\,dx \] when the density is supported on the whole line, or from \(0\) when the support is the positive reals. Then \(P(x>y)=1-P(x<y)\). The PDF is the derivative of the CDF.

Figure: CDF (thick) and PDF (thin) for the heights of computer science students.

Sample-Based Approximations

The expectation of \(f(k)\) under \(P(k)\) is \(\langle f(k)\rangle=\sum_k f(k)P(k)\). From the pictorial definition, \[ P(k)=\lim_{S\to\infty}\frac{s_k}{S}, \] where \(S\) is the number of independent trials and \(s_k\) the number that landed in state \(k\). For large \(S\), \[ \langle f(k)\rangle \approx \frac{1}{S}\sum_{i=1}^{S} f(k_i), \] with \(k_i\) independent samples from \(P(k)\). The sample mean \(\bar k = S^{-1}\sum_i k_i\) is the special case \(f(k)=k\). The same replacement of integrals by averages holds for densities.

Most texts use “mean” for both the sample mean and the mean of the distribution. Keep the distinction. Later in the course we will also meet time averages, and we will assume that a time average equals an average under a steady-state distribution.

Mean of a Poisson

The mean of \(P(k\mid\mu)=\mu^k e^{-\mu}/k!\) is \(\mu\). Pull \(\mu e^{-\mu}\) out of the sum, cancel \(k/k!\), shift the index \(m=k-1\), and recognise the series for \(e^{\mu}\). The variance is an exercise on Tutorial Sheet 1.

rng = np.random.default_rng(1)
k = rng.poisson(mu, size=20_000)
print("sample mean", k.mean(), "sample variance", k.var())
