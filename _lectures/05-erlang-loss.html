Erlang Loss: \(M/M/m/m\)

Erlang delay holds overflowing customers. Erlang loss does not: if all \(m\) servers are busy the arrival is rejected. Kendall writes \(M/M/m/m\) — \(m\) servers and system capacity \(m\), so the waiting line has length zero. Offered load is still \(a=\lambda/\mu\), but carried load \(a'\) is smaller because some traffic is blocked, as in \(M/M/1/m\).

Figure: Flow chart of the Erlang loss system: offered load \(a\) in, carried load \(a'<a\) out.

Figure: Markov chain for \(M/M/m/m\): the chain stops at \(m\); there is no queueing state.

Birth-death rates are \[ \lambda_k=\begin{cases} \lambda & 0\le k<m,\\ 0 & k\ge m, \end{cases} \qquad \mu_k=k\mu\quad(1\le k\le m). \] The value of \(\mu_k\) for \(k>m\) is irrelevant: those states are unreachable.

Truncated Poisson

Interior detailed balance is the \(M/M/\infty\) relation, \[ \lambda P(k-1)=k\mu P(k)\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad P(k)=\frac{a}{k}P(k-1)=\frac{a^k}{k!}P(0). \] There is no \(k>m\) region to match. Normalisation over \(k=0,\ldots,m\) produces the truncated Poisson \[ P(0)=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{m}\frac{a^n}{n!}\right)^{-1},\qquad P(k)=\frac{a^k/k!}{\sum_{n=0}^{m}a^n/n!}. \] As \(m\to\infty\) this recovers \(M/M/\infty\), as it must.

Figure: Boundary flow for \(k\le m\): \(\lambda P(k-1)=k\mu P(k)\).

Probability of Blocking

Waiting probability is zero: the chain never enters \(k>m\). The quantity of interest is the blocking probability, which by PASTA is \(P(m)\), \[ B(m,a)=P(m)=\frac{a^m/m!}{\sum_{n=0}^{m}a^n/n!}. \] This is Erlang’s first formula, or Erlang B. The same overflow caution as Erlang C applies; npa_plots.erlang_b evaluates it in log space, matching erlangB.m.

Carried Load and Utilisation

Carried load is offered load times the probability of not being blocked, \[ a'=a\bigl(1-B(m,a)\bigr). \] Trunk occupancy — utilisation of a line — is then \(\rho=a'/m\), not \(a/m\). In a lossless system \(a'=a\) and the two coincide. The throughput of the system, the rate at which customers actually enter service, is \[ \dot{\lambda}=\lambda\bigl(1-P(m)\bigr). \] Little’s formula on a lossy system must use this carried rate, not the offered arrival rate: blocked customers never enter, so they do not contribute to occupancy.
