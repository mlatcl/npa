Erlang Delay: \(M/M/m\)

\(M/M/\infty\) is the unlimited-server extreme. A call centre, a trunk group with holding, or any pool of \(m\) identical exponential servers with a waiting line is the Erlang delay system: Kendall \(M/M/m\). Arrivals that find all servers busy join an unbounded queue. The system is lossless, so carried load equals offered load \(a=\lambda/\mu\). The mean holding time is \(\tau=1/\mu\equiv\bar{X}\).

Figure: Schematic of Erlang delay: offered load \(a\) enters through a queue; nothing is lost, so carried load equals \(a\).

Figure: State diagram of \(M/M/m\): death rate climbs as \(k\mu\) until \(k=m\), then saturates at \(m\mu\).

Birth-death rates are \[ \lambda_k=\lambda,\qquad \mu_k=\begin{cases} k\mu & 1\le k\le m,\\ m\mu & k>m. \end{cases} \] Utilisation of the server pool is \(\rho=\lambda/(m\mu)=a/m\). Stability still requires \(\rho<1\), i.e. \(a<m\): the arrival rate must be less than the full-pool service rate.

Detailed Balance

For \(k\le m\) the boundary is the \(M/M/\infty\) boundary, \[ \lambda P(k-1)=k\mu P(k)\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad P(k)=\frac{m\rho}{k}P(k-1)=\frac{(m\rho)^k}{k!}P(0). \]

Figure: Boundary flow while at least one server is free: \(\lambda P(k-1)=k\mu P(k)\).

For \(k>m\) every server is busy, so the death rate is \(m\mu\), \[ \lambda P(k-1)=m\mu P(k)\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad P(k)=\rho P(k-1)=\rho^{k-m}P(m). \] With \(P(m)=(m\rho)^m P(0)/m!\) this is \[ P(k)=\frac{m^m\rho^k}{m!}P(0),\qquad k>m. \]

Figure: Boundary flow once all \(m\) servers are busy: \(\lambda P(k-1)=m\mu P(k)\).

The stationary distribution is therefore piecewise \[ P(k)=\begin{cases} P(0)\,(m\rho)^k/k! & k\le m,\\ P(0)\,m^m\rho^k/m! & k>m. \end{cases} \] Normalisation, summing the Poisson-like head and the geometric tail, gives \[ P(0)=\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(m\rho)^k}{k!}+\frac{(m\rho)^m}{m!(1-\rho)}\right]^{-1}. \]

Probability of Waiting

The operational question for \(M/M/m\) is the probability that an arrival finds every server busy and must wait, \[ P_Q=P(k\ge m)=\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}P(k)=P(0)\frac{(m\rho)^m}{m!(1-\rho)}\doteq C(m,a). \] This is Erlang’s second formula, or Erlang C. In offered-load form \(a=m\rho\), \[ C(m,a)=\frac{P(0)\,a^m}{m!(1-a/m)}. \]

Direct evaluation of \(a^m/m!\) overflows for large \(m\). Compute in log space, \[ \ln C(m,a)=\ln P(0)+m\ln a-\ln m!-\ln(1-a/m), \] with \(\ln m!=\ln\Gamma(m+1)\). The original MATLAB helpers erlangC.m / plotErlang.m do that; npa_plots.erlang_c and npa_plots.plot_erlang_curves are the same calculation.

Mean Occupancy and Waiting Time

The mean number in the system splits into the mean queue length and the mean number of busy servers \(a=m\rho\), \[ \bar{N}=\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}C(m,a)+m\rho,\qquad \bar{N}_Q=\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}C(m,a). \] Little’s formula on the waiting line, with arrival rate \(\lambda\) (nothing is lost), gives the mean wait for all customers, \[ \bar{W}=\frac{\bar{N}_Q}{\lambda}=\frac{C(m,a)}{m\mu(1-\rho)}. \] Customers who do not queue have \(\bar{W}_{\sim Q}=0\), so the mean wait among those who do queue is \[ \bar{W}=C(m,a)\,\bar{W}_Q\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \bar{W}_Q=\frac{1}{m\mu(1-\rho)}=\frac{1}{\mu(m-a)}. \] That conditional wait does not depend on \(C(m,a)\).
