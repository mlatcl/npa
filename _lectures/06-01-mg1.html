\(M/G/1\) and Pollaczek–Khinchin

If service times are generally distributed we cannot use a birth-death process. The tool is mean residual service time, and it yields the Pollaczek–Khinchin formula: expected wait for any service-time distribution with finite variance, \[ \bar{W}=\frac{\lambda\langle X^2\rangle}{2(1-\rho)}, \] where \(\rho=\lambda\bar{X}\) and \(\bar{X}=\langle X\rangle\).

Assumptions

The derivation uses three assumptions. The queue is FIFO. Arrival times are statistically independent of service times. Service times have finite variance. (The notes originally wrote ``arrival-time variance’’; the 2005 paper, and the residual-time argument itself, require finite service-time variance.)

Mean residual service time \(\bar{R}\) is the leftover service seen by an arrival. Under those assumptions \[ \bar{W}=\frac{\bar{R}}{1-\rho}. \] The rest of the argument is an expression for \(\bar{R}\).

Residual Time from Completions

Figure: Residual service time over a busy period: a sawtooth of triangles with sides \(X_i\). \(M(t)\) is the number of completions in \([0,t]\).

The area under the residual-time curve up to \(t\) is \[ A(t)=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{M(t)}X_i^2. \] The time average is \[ \frac{A(t)}{t}=\frac12\frac{M(t)}{t}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{M(t)}X_i^2}{M(t)}. \] As \(t\to\infty\), \(M(t)/t\to\lambda\) and the sample second moment converges (finite variance), so \[ \bar{R}=\frac12\lambda\langle X^2\rangle. \] Substitute into \(\bar{W}=\bar{R}/(1-\rho)\) to recover Pollaczek–Khinchin. Sojourn time is then \(\bar{T}=\bar{X}+\bar{W}\), and Little gives \(\bar{N}_Q=\lambda\bar{W}\) and \(\bar{N}=\lambda\bar{T}\). This derivation was Question 1 of the 2002–03 paper.

Residual Time from an Arrival

Figure: The \(i\)th arrival sees residual \(R_i\) on the customer in service and waiting times \(X\) for each of the \(k_i-1\) customers already queued.

Write \(W_i\) for the wait of the \(i\)th customer, \(R_i\) for the residual it sees, \(X_i\) for its own service, and \(k_i\) for the occupancy on arrival. Then \[ W_i=R_i+\sum_{j=1}^{k_i-1}X_{(j)}. \] Average over the first \(I\) arrivals and let \(I\to\infty\). Time averages equal steady-state means, and independence of the \(i\)th arrival from the work already present, together with Little on the queue, recover \(\bar{W}=\bar{R}/(1-\rho)\).

Special Cases

Second moment \(\langle X^2\rangle=\mathrm{Var}(X)+\bar{X}^2\). For exponential service, \(\bar{X}=1/\mu\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=1/\mu^2\), so \(\langle X^2\rangle=2/\mu^2\) and Pollaczek–Khinchin collapses to the \(M/M/1\) wait \(\bar{W}=\rho/(\mu(1-\rho))\).

Deterministic service (\(M/D/1\), fixed-length packets on a fixed-bandwidth line) has \(\mathrm{Var}(X)=0\), so \(\langle X^2\rangle=1/\mu^2\) and \[ \bar{W}=\frac{\rho}{2\mu(1-\rho)}, \] half the \(M/M/1\) wait. Among distributions with a given mean, deterministic service gives the lowest delay: the residual sawtooth has the smallest area.

Uniform service on \([0,b]\) has mean \(b/2\) and variance \(b^2/12\). With \(\mu=2/b\), the second moment is \(4/(3\mu^2)\) and \[ \bar{W}=\frac{2\rho}{3\mu(1-\rho)}. \]

Writing \(\mu=1/\bar{X}\): exponential wait \(\rho/(\mu(1-\rho))\), sojourn \(1/(\mu(1-\rho))\), \(\bar{N}=\rho/(1-\rho)\); deterministic wait half of that, sojourn \((2-\rho)/(2\mu(1-\rho))\), \(\bar{N}=\rho(2-\rho)/(2(1-\rho))\).

\(M/G/1\) with Vacations

When the server is free it may attend to other work — control traffic, a kitchen check — a vacation. An arrival that finds the server on vacation waits until the vacation ends. If a vacation ends and the server is still idle, it takes another. Vacation lengths \(V_1,V_2,\ldots\) are i.i.d. and independent of arrivals and service.

Figure: An \(M/G/1\) server that takes a vacation whenever the queue is empty.

Figure: Residual time now includes vacation triangles as well as service triangles.

The residual-time integral now has two families of triangles. With \(M(t)\) service completions and \(L(t)\) vacations in \([0,t]\), \[ \frac1t\int_0^t r(\tau)\,d\tau =\frac{M(t)}{t}\frac12\frac{\sum X_i^2}{M(t)} +\frac{L(t)}{t}\frac12\frac{\sum V_i^2}{L(t)}. \] The fraction of time available for vacations is \(1-\rho\), so \(L(t)=t(1-\rho)/\bar{V}_t\). The limit is \[ \bar{R}=\frac12\lambda\langle X^2\rangle+\frac{(1-\rho)\langle V^2\rangle}{2\bar{V}}, \] and therefore \[ \bar{W}=\frac{\lambda\langle X^2\rangle}{2(1-\rho)}+\frac{\langle V^2\rangle}{2\bar{V}}. \] Pollaczek–Khinchin plus a vacation residual that does not depend on \(\rho\).

A bar with Poisson arrivals, exponential service of mean \(\bar{X}\), and exponential kitchen checks of mean \(\bar{V}\) has \(\langle X^2\rangle=2\bar{X}^2\) and \(\langle V^2\rangle=2\bar{V}^2\), so \[ \bar{W}=\frac{\rho\bar{X}}{1-\rho}+\bar{V}. \] Deterministic vacations of the same mean would add only \(\bar{V}/2\).
