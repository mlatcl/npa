Tutorial 3

Erlang delay and loss, and the \(M/G/1\) queue. Assume Poisson arrivals and exponential service in the first two questions.

A complaints call centre

A telephone call centre handles complaints from bank customers. In the busy hour, calls arrive at 120 customers per hour. Dealing with each customer takes 3 minutes on average. When all assistants are occupied, customers are placed in a holding system.

  1. The centre has 10 assistants. What is the probability that a customer has to join the queue?

  2. What is the expected queueing time for each customer?

  3. Customers who do not queue have waiting time 0. What is the average waiting time for those who do queue?

  4. The bank wishes to combine two such centres. Both have busy-hour arrival rate 120 customers per hour. How many staff does the combined centre need to keep the queueing probability at least as low as in (1)?

  5. Answer (2) and (3) for the new system.

Trunk lines

A telephone company plans to rent trunk lines between Birmingham and London for $1{,}000 per year. They expect a demand of 20 calls per hour and an average holding time of 15 minutes. They can charge $0.01 per minute per customer. There is no holding facility.

  1. How many trunk lines are needed to keep the blocking probability below 0.01?

  2. How much profit will they make?

A triangular service time

A service-time distribution is \[ P(X)=\begin{cases} \frac{2}{b}-\frac{2}{b^2}X & 0<X\le b,\\ 0 & X>b. \end{cases} \]

  1. Sketch this distribution.

  2. The Pollaczek–Khinchin formula gives the mean waiting time \[ \bar{W}=\frac{\lambda\langle X^2\rangle}{2(1-\rho)}. \]

  3. By determining the first two moments \(\langle X\rangle\) and \(\langle X^2\rangle\), derive the average waiting time for the queue.
