Falling leaves

Leaves fall from a tree as a Poisson process at a rate of one every minute.

  1. You arrive at 16:00. During the next ten minutes, what is the probability that you see twelve leaves fall?

  2. What is the probability that you see \(n\) leaves fall for \(n=0,\ldots,20\)? Plot the results.

from scipy.stats import poisson
lam, t = 1.0, 10.0
n = np.arange(0, 21)
p = poisson.pmf(n, lam * t)
print("P(k=12) =", poisson.pmf(12, lam * t))
plt.stem(n, p)
plt.xlabel("$n$")
plt.ylabel(r"$P(n\mid\lambda t=10)$")
plt.show()
  1. Where does the curve peak? Compare the peak with the expected value \(\lambda t\).

Poisson second moment

Show that \[ \langle n^2\rangle=\lambda t\, e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n(\lambda t)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}, \] where the expectation is under a Poisson distribution. Then, substituting \(m=n-1\), show that \[ \langle n^2\rangle=(\lambda t)^2+\lambda t. \] What, therefore, is the variance of a Poisson distribution?

Hint. There are similarities with the proof of the Poisson mean from the lecture.

Gaussian samples

This question needs a computer. Draw \(N\) samples \(\{x_n\}_{n=1}^N\) from a Gaussian with mean 0 and variance 1 (numpy.random.randn is the old MATLAB randn). For each \(N\) in the table, compute the sample mean and the sample second moment.

\(N\) 10 50 100 200 500 1000
\(\frac{1}{N}\sum_n x_n\)
\(\frac{1}{N}\sum_n x_n^2\) 
Ns = [10, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000]
rng = np.random.default_rng(1)
for N in Ns:
    x = rng.normal(size=N)
    print(N, x.mean(), np.mean(x**2))

  1. What is the true mean of the distribution you have sampled from?

  2. For each \(N\), plot the squared difference between the sample mean and the true mean. What sort of trend do the points give? Is that trend consistent? When, typically, are the sample estimates of the mean worst?

  3. What is the true second moment of the distribution?

  4. For each \(N\), plot the squared difference between the sample second moment and the true second moment. Is the trend similar to that of the sample mean? Are the second-moment estimates typically better or worse? Why?

Hint. Repeat the experiment several times and summarise with means and standard deviations. matplotlib.pyplot.errorbar replaces MATLAB errorbar.

The exponential distribution

Compute the mean and variance of \[ p(x)=\lambda e^{-\lambda x},\qquad 0<x<\infty. \]

Repeat the sampling experiments of the previous question with an exponential of rate \(\lambda=3\), using npa_plots.exponential_sample in place of the old exponentialSamp.m.

lam = 3.0
Ns = [10, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000]
for N in Ns:
    x = npa_plots.exponential_sample(lam, size=N, rng=1)
    print(N, x.mean(), np.mean(x**2))
