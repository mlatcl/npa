Tutorial 2

Network Performance Analysis. \(M/M/1\), Little’s formula, and the exponential distribution.

The exponential density

The \(M/M/1\) queue uses exponential densities for interarrival times and service times. The interarrival density with parameter \(\lambda\) is \[ p(t)=\lambda e^{-\lambda t},\qquad t\ge 0. \]

  1. Sketch this density function.

  2. Show that both the mean and the standard deviation are \(1/\lambda\).

  3. Briefly explain what these two properties of a density represent.

  4. What is meant by the memoryless property of the exponential distribution?

Occupancy variance

In the notes the mean number of customers in an \(M/M/1\) system is \[ \langle k\rangle=\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}. \] Show that the variance of \(k\) is \[ \langle k^2\rangle-\langle k\rangle^2=\frac{\rho}{(1-\rho)^2}. \]

Delay versus utilisation

For an \(M/M/1\) queue, sketch the following quantities against the utilisation \(\rho\).

  1. The average number of customers in the system.

  2. The average time delay of the system.

  3. Using these plots, comment on the consequences of allowing \(\rho\) to approach 1.

A cash machine

Students arrive as a Poisson process at a cash-dispensing machine with average interarrival time 5 minutes. The time a student spends on the machine is exponential with mean 2 minutes.

  1. What is the probability that a student arriving at the machine will have to wait?

  2. What is the average waiting (queueing) time for a student?

  3. The bank plans to install a second machine when the average waiting time is 5 minutes or longer. At what average arrival rate will this occur? For that arrival rate, what is the total average delay that students spend at the machine?

  4. What is the average queue length at that arrival rate?

Finite waiting room

For a single server (\(M/M/1\) or \(M/M/1/m\)) the utilisation \(\rho\) is the probability that the occupancy is not zero. For a lossless system, Little’s formula (valid for \(M/G/1\)) gave \(\rho=\lambda/\mu\). Use the stationary distribution of the \(M/M/1/m\) queue to compute the utilisation, and explain why it is not \(\lambda/\mu\).
