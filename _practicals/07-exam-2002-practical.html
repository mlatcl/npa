Paper

Network Performance Analysis. Autumn semester 2002–2003. Two hours. Press mark COM6131 (masters) / COM3270 (undergraduate).

This paper examines \(M/G/1\) residual time (lectures 11–13), ns-2 and Kleinrock (Lab 2; lectures 14–15), Erlang C and Little (lectures 6 and 9), and a finite multi-server birth-death chain (lectures 5, 8–10).

Question 1

In an \(M/G/1\) queue inter-arrival times are exponentially distributed and service times are generally distributed.

(a) [50%] Use the concept of residual service time to show that, assuming customers are served in the order that they arrive, the average waiting time is \[ \bar{W}=\frac{\bar{R}}{1-\rho}, \] where \(\bar{R}\) is the average residual service time and \(\rho\) is the utilisation. Use diagrams where necessary, and state your assumptions clearly.

(b) [40%] Use a graphical argument to derive a formula for the residual service time, then combine it with (a) to show that \[ \bar{W}=\frac{\lambda\langle X^2\rangle}{2(1-\rho)}, \] where \(\lambda\) is the arrival rate and \(\langle X^2\rangle\) is the second moment of service time.

(c) [10%] This is the Pollaczek–Khinchin formula. Use it to derive the average waiting time in an \(M/D/1\) queue.

Question 2

You are presented with the following portion of Tcl designed for ns-2.

set node1 [$ns node] set node2 [$ns node] set node3 [$ns node] set node4 [$ns node]

$node1 set multiPath_ 1

set link1 [$ns duplex-link $node1 $node2 1Kb 0ms DropTail] set link2 [$ns duplex-link $node1 $node3 1Kb 0ms DropTail] set link3 [$ns duplex-link $node2 $node4 1Kb 0ms DropTail] set link4 [$ns duplex-link $node3 $node4 1Kb 0ms DropTail]

set src [new Agent/UDP] $ns attach-agent $node1 $src set sink [new Agent/Null] $ns attach-agent $node4 $sink

(a) [25%] Assuming that only the specified nodes exist, draw the network topology showing the associated routes.

(b) [35%] State the assumptions behind the Kleinrock independence approximation. Discuss to what extent they are valid for this topology.

(c) [25%] Packet arrivals at node1 are Poisson at rate 2 per second. Packet lengths are exponential with mean 0.5 Kb. Packets are sent randomly along all possible routes with equal probability. Use Kleinrock’s independence assumption to estimate the steady-state average number of packets at each link.

(d) [15%] What is the expected delay for each route through the network?

Question 3

Hand in the Erlang C curves provided with your answer to this question.

Northern Telecom has purchased several local telecommunications companies, each with their own regional support team. There are three regional teams. Calls arrive as a Poisson process; service times are exponential. Region A has \(m=10\) and 120 customers per hour; region B has \(m=13\) and 160 customers per hour; region C has \(m=10\) and 100 customers per hour.

Figure: Erlang C curves issued with the paper (offered traffic 0 to 20).

(a) [30%] Average holding time \(\tau\) is 3 minutes. Use the Erlang C curves to give, for each region, the probability of queueing \(C(m,a)\).

(b) [30%] The mean queue length in \(M/M/m\) is \(\bar{N}_Q=\rho C(m,a)/(1-\rho)\). Use Little’s formula to obtain the mean queueing time, and compute it for each centre.

(c) [20%] The mean queueing time for customers who do not queue is zero. What is the mean queueing time for customers who do queue?

(d) [20%] The company merges the centres to one location. Total traffic, holding time, and staff numbers stay the same. What is the average queueing time for the new centre?

Question 4

A call centre employs thirty operators selling tickets. It has sixty lines in total. When all operators are active, new calls join a queue for the first free operator. Model the centre as \(M/M/m/n\): \(m\) servers and maximum occupancy \(n\).

(a) [30%] Draw a state diagram for the system.

(b) [20%] Write down the detailed balance equations for \(k<m\) and for \(m\le k<n\).

(c) [30%] For each of those two regions, write the steady-state distribution as a function of \(P(0)\).

(d) [20%] Using normalisation, show that \[ P(0)=\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{a^k}{k!}+\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\frac{a^k}{m!\,m^{k-m}}\right]^{-1}, \] where \(a=\lambda/\mu\).
