Rooms

From the 2006–07 notes. Mondays 15:10–17:00 (St Georges LT17). Tuesdays 16:10–17:00 (St Georges LT2). Lab classes in the Lewin Lab on the Mondays of weeks 3 and 8 (the notes also mention a week-9 lab review of ns-2).

Calendar

Monday is a two-hour slot; Tuesday is one hour. Lecture numbers are those used in the notes.

Week 1. Monday lectures 1–2, networks and probability review. Tuesday lecture 3, Poisson processes.

Week 2. Monday lectures 4–5, birth-death processes and \(M/M/1\). Tuesday lecture 6, Little’s formula.

Week 3. Monday lab class 1, discrete simulation of \(M/M/1\). Tuesday tutorial 1, probability and Poisson processes.

Week 4. Monday lectures 7–8, other Markov queues and Erlang delay. Tuesday lab review 1.

Week 5. Monday lectures 9–10, Erlang loss and Markov queues review. Tuesday tutorial 2, \(M/M/1\) and Little’s formula.

Week 6. Monday lectures 11–12, \(M/G/1\) and vacations. Tuesday lecture 13, \(M/G/1\) continued (ARQ).

Week 7. Monday lectures 14–15, networks of queues and Jackson’s theorem. Tuesday tutorial 3, Erlang delay and loss, and \(M/G/1\).

Week 8. Monday lab class 2, simulation of queues in ns-2. Tuesday lecture 16, ALOHA.

Week 9. Tuesday lab review 2, the ns-2 lab.

Week 10. Exam practice: the 2004–05 paper.

Week 12. Monday exam review of that paper.

Evaluation

By 2006–07 the course was 100% exam. In 2001–02 the discrete \(M/M/1\) simulation (Lab 1) was 30% coursework, due 21 November 2002, with code recovered from a DCS directory COM6131a1y1 or COM3270a1y1.

Reading list

The course is based primarily on Bertsekas and Gallager. The notes cover in extra detail areas of importance for the course and add material where the text is thin (simulation in particular).

Campus-only material originally sat at http://www.dcs.shef.ac.uk/~neil/campus_only/com6131/. The public 2005–06 snapshot is under published/ in this repository.

These notes

The first three chapters — introduction and networks, probability review, Poisson processes — are background, not the syllabus as such. The syllabus itself starts at the Poisson process and the Markov material that follows.

Further Reading

  • Chapter 1, background and review; Chapter 3, Sections 3.1–3.5, queueing models; Chapter 4 in less detail, Sections 4.1–4.4, 4.5.2, 4.5.3 of Bertsekas and Gallager (1992)

  • Standard introductory networks text; little coverage of queueing of Tanenbaum (2003)

  • Alternative text for details on queues of Chan (2000)
