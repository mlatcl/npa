How this lab sits in the course

This is the Monday of week 3, immediately after Birth-Death Processes and the \(M/M/1\) Queue (lectures 4 and 5) and Little’s Formula (lecture 6). The lectures derived \(P(k)=(1-\rho)\rho^k\) from detailed balance and showed a discrete-time chain with \(\Delta t\) as a computational stand-in for the continuous process. This lab asks you to write that chain, sample it, and see when the sample mean is a trustworthy estimate of \(\bar{N}=\rho/(1-\rho)\). Tutorial 2, later the same week, returns to occupancy variance and Little on paper.

The task

Use time steps \(\Delta t=0.01\). The 2006 sheet used MATLAB (hist, bar, rand, and the demo simulateQueue.m). Write the loop yourself first. npa_plots.simulate_mm1 is the same algorithm, for checking.

1. Run the chain

Code a simulation with birth rate \(\lambda=1\) for all \(k=0,1,\ldots\) and death rate \(\mu=2\) for \(k=1,1,\ldots\) (with \(\mu_0=0\)). At each step the probability of \(+1\) is \(\lambda\Delta t\), of \(-1\) is \(\mu\Delta t\), and of no change is \(1-(\lambda+\mu)\Delta t\). Run for \(1000\) seconds (\(100{,}000\) iterations). Store the occupancy at every step: those stored values are samples from the model the queue represents.

While you wait for simulations to run you might like to start looking at the questions below.

occupancy = npa_plots.simulate_mm1(1.0, 2.0, delta_t=0.01, n_iters=100_000, rng=1)
print(occupancy[:20], occupancy.mean())

2. Normalized histogram

Produce a normalized histogram of the samples (the area of the bars adds up to one, not to the number of counts).

3. Sample mean

Compute the mean of the samples.

4. Steady-state probabilities

Use the detailed balance equations to derive the steady-state probabilities for each state in your queueing system. Comment on the similarities between those equations and those of the \(M/M/1\) queue from the lecture.

5. Analytical mean

Use these steady-state probabilities to derive the distribution’s mean.

6. Compare

Plot the computed probabilities from (4) against the normalized histogram from (2). Compare the sample mean from (3) with the analytical mean from (5). Comment on differences between the analytically computed values and those computed from the samples.

fig, ax = npa_plots.plot_mm1_occupancy(1.0, 2.0, n_iters=100_000, rng=1)
ax.set_title(r"$\lambda=1$, $\mu=2$, $\rho=0.5$, 1000 seconds")
plt.show()

7. High utilisation

Run the model again with \(\lambda=1.9\), keeping \(\mu=2\). Repeat (2) and (3). Are the sample-based estimates more or less accurate for this system? Comment on why.

8. What simulation can hide

Real networks are more complex than this chain. Many cannot be studied analytically, so their statistics must be determined through simulation. Summarise what you have learned from the simple example above about the possible dangers of such simulations.
