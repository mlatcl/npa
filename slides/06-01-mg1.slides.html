  • This gives the expected queueing time for any distribution of service times with finite variance.

Pollaczek-Khinchin formula

  • The Pollaczek-Khinchin formula is given as

Pollaczek-Khinchin formula (continued)

  • \(\bar{W}\) is expected waiting time,
  • \(\left\langle X^{2}\right\rangle =\left\langle X^{2}\right\rangle _{p(X)}\) is the second moment under the service time distribution,
  • utilisation factor is given as \(\rho=\lambda\bar{X}\) and
  • \(\bar{X}=\left\langle X\right\rangle _{p(X)}\) is the mean under the service time distribution.

Proof of Pollaczek-Khinchin Formula

  • Proof of the Pollaczek-Khinchin formula.
  • In our proof we will make three assumptions
  • The queue is FIFO queue, i.e.customers are served in the order they arrive. This assumption may be relaxed but it will simplify our proof.

Proof of Pollaczek-Khinchin Formula (continued)

  • The arrival times are statistically independent of the service times.
  • The service time distribution has a variance which is finite.
  • To formulate the proof we introduce a concept known as the mean residual service time.

The Residual Service Time

  • The residual service time is … the amount of service time that remains for the customer under service from the perspective of a customer who has just arrived in the queue.
  • The first part of the proof occurs from the perspective of the arriving customer.
  • The arriving customer is considered to be the \(i\)th customer.

Wait for ith Customer

Schematic representation of residual service time.

Wait for ith Customer (continued)

  • From the figure the wait for the \(i\)th customer will be given by\[ W_{i}=R_{i}+\sum_{j=i-(k_{i}-1)}^{i-1}X_{j}\]
  • Summing over both sides (i.e.for all the arriving customers up to the \(I\)th) \[ \sum_{i=1}^{I}W_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}R_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{j=i-(k_{i}-1)}^{i-1}X_{j}\]
  • Dividing through by \(I\) gives us \[ \sum_{i=1}^{I}\frac{W_{i}}{I}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\frac{R_{i}}{I}+\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{j=i-(k_{i}-1)}^{i-1}\frac{X_{j}}{I}\]
  • In the limit as \(I\rightarrow\infty\) this becomes \[ \lim_{I\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\frac{W_{i}}{I}=\lim_{I\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\frac{R_{i}}{I}+\lim_{I\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\frac{(k_{i}-1)X_{i}}{I}\]
  • The terms of which are recognised as time averages.

Time Average Equivalence

  • If we assume time averages are equivalent to steady state averages this gives \[ \left\langle W\right\rangle =\left\langle R\right\rangle +\left\langle \left(k-1\right)X\right\rangle .\]
  • The length of the queue as seen by the \(i\)th arrival is independent of the \(i\)th arrival’s length (our second assumption)

Time Average Equivalence (continued)

  • this means $(k-1)X=k-1X$ and since \((k-1)\) is the length of the queue we may write \[ \bar{W}=\bar{R}+\bar{N}_{Q}\bar{X}\]
  • Using Little’s theorem for the queue we have\[ \bar{N}_{Q}=\lambda\bar{W}\]
  • Substituting back in we have\[ \bar{W}=\bar{R}+\lambda\bar{X}\bar{W}.\]

Wait in Terms of Residual Service Time

  • From the previous slide:\[ \bar{W}=\bar{R}+\lambda\bar{X}\bar{W}.\]
  • And the fact that \(\rho=\lambda\bar{X}\) (which stems from Little’s formula for the service) gives us \[ \bar{W}=\bar{R}+\rho\bar{W}\]

Residual Service Time

  • The average service time is the area divided by time, \(t\), \[ A(t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{M(t)}X_{i}^{2}.\] & = & \ & = & .

  • As \(t\rightarrow\infty\), if the variance is finite, we find that \(M(t)\rightarrow\infty\) and we obtain\[ \bar{R}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda\left\langle X^{2}\right\rangle .\]

Pollaczek-Khinchin Formula

  • Again assuming that time averages and ensemble averages are equal we substitute back into (\(\ref{eqn:arrivals}\)) to obtain the Pollaczek-Khinchin formula \[ \bar{W}=\frac{\lambda\left\langle X^{2}\right\rangle }{2(1-\rho)}.\]
  • This implies that the total waiting time in the system is \[ \bar{T}=\bar{X}+\frac{\lambda\left\langle X^{2}\right\rangle }{2(1-\rho)}\] and through Little’s formula we can obtain the expected queue length, \(\bar{N}_{Q}\), and the expected number in the system, \(\bar{N}\).

Sanity Check — Markov Service times

  • The exponentially distributed service times, \[ P\left(X\right)=\mu\exp\left(-\mu X\right),\]have a mean of \[ \bar{X}=\frac{1}{\mu}\] and a variance of \(\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\) leading to a second moment\[ \left\langle X^{2}\right\rangle =\frac{2}{\mu^{2}}\]
  • Substituting into the Pollaczek-Khinchin formula we obtain {W} & = & \ & = & which is identical to those we derived for the \(M/M/1\) queue.

Deterministic Service — \(M/D/1\)

  • Some systems have deterministic service times. For example, transmission of fixed length packets over a channel with a fixed bandwidth.
  • We represent a `deterministic distribution’ as\[ P\left(X\right)=\delta\left(X-\frac{1}{\mu}\right)\]where \(\delta\left(\cdot\right)\)is the Dirac delta function.
  • For this system \[ \left\langle X\right\rangle =\frac{1}{\mu}\]and \[ \left\langle X^{2}\right\rangle =\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\]i.e. the variance is zero.

P-K Formula for Deterministic Systems

  • For this system \[ \left\langle X\right\rangle =\frac{1}{\mu}\]and \[ \left\langle X^{2}\right\rangle =\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\]i.e. the variance is zero.
  • Substituting into the Pollaczek-Khinchin formula we obtain \[ \bar{W}=\frac{\rho}{2\mu\left(1-\rho\right)}.\]
