Birth-Death Processes and the M/M/1 Queue

2006-10-02

Markov Chains

  • Markov chain: discrete time
  • Markov process: continuous time
  • Markov property: future \(\perp\) past \(\mid\) present

The Markov Property

Monopoly

A Monopoly board. The token’s square after a turn depends only on the square before the turn and the dice.
  • Forty squares; \(N(t)\) counts circuits as well as the square
  • \(N(t)=N(t-1)+d(t)\)
  • Markov: the next square does not depend on the path that led here

State Diagrams

  • State diagrams: nodes are values of \(N\)
  • Transitions carry probabilities or rates
  • The state space may be infinite

The Poisson Process as a Markov Process

Coin Monopoly

State diagram for Coin Monopoly. Each node is a square index. With each turn the token stays or moves one step, with the probabilities on the arrows.
  • Coin Monopoly: \(d(t)\in\{0,1\}\)
  • \(P(h)=\lambda\Delta t\), turn length \(\Delta t\)
  • After \(n\) turns the occupancy is binomial

Binomial Trials to a Poisson Process

  • \(n=t/\Delta t\), \(P(h)=\lambda t/n\)
  • \(n\to\infty\) recovers \(P(k\mid\lambda,t)=(\lambda t)^k e^{-\lambda t}/k!\)
  • Poisson process \(=\) Markov process with only \(k\to k+1\)

Process State Diagrams

Coin Monopoly in the limit \(\Delta t\to 0\): a Poisson process. Rates replace probabilities, and there are no self-loops.

Steady State

  • Steady state: no net change, or balanced flows
  • We ask for delay and occupancy after the transient

Birth-Death Processes

State diagram for a discrete-time birth-death chain.
State diagram for a continuous-time birth-death process. Births increase \(k\); deaths decrease it.
  • Birth: \(k\to k+1\); death: \(k\to k-1\)
  • \(k\) cannot go negative
  • Many queues are birth-death processes

Kendall’s Notation

  • \(A/S/m/K\): arrivals / service / servers / capacity
  • \(M\): Poisson or exponential
  • \(D\): deterministic; \(G\): general

Global Balance

Discrete Time

Continuous Time

  • Global balance: flow out of \(k\) equals flow into \(k\)
  • Chain: \(P(j)\sum_i P_{ji}=\sum_i P(i)P_{ij}\)
  • Process: rates \(q_{ij}\) in place of \(P_{ij}\)

Detailed Balance

Flow across the boundary between \(k-1\) and \(k\). At equilibrium \(\lambda_{k-1}P(k-1)=\mu_k P(k)\).
  • Neighbours only \(\Rightarrow\) detailed balance
  • \(\lambda_{k-1}P(k-1)=\mu_k P(k)\)
  • \(P(k)=(\lambda_{k-1}/\mu_k)P(k-1)\)

The \(M/M/1\) Queue

State diagram for the \(M/M/1\) queue: unbounded \(k\), no transition below \(0\), constant rates.
Boundary flow for \(M/M/1\): \(\lambda P(k-1)=\mu P(k)\).
  • \(M/M/1\): \(\lambda_k=\lambda\), \(\mu_k=\mu\)
  • \(\rho=\lambda/\mu\), \(P(k)=\rho^k(1-\rho)\) for \(\rho<1\)
  • \(\rho\ge 1\): no stationary distribution

Mean Occupancy

  • \(\langle k\rangle=\rho/(1-\rho)=\lambda/(\mu-\lambda)\)
  • Includes the customer in service
  • \(\mathrm{Var}(k)=\rho/(1-\rho)^2\) (Tutorial 2)

Discrete Simulation of \(M/M/1\)

Discrete-time \(M/M/1\): from occupancy \(k\) the next occupancy is \(k+1\), \(k-1\), or \(k\).
Draw \(U\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)\). Birth if \(U<P(b)\); death if \(P(b)\le U<P(b)+P(d)\) and \(k>0\); otherwise stay.
  • Three outcomes per step: \(+1\), \(-1\), or stay
  • \(P(b)=\lambda\Delta t\), \(P(d)=\mu\Delta t\)
  • Small \(\Delta t\) approximates the continuous queue
