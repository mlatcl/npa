Networks of Queues and Jackson’s Theorem

2006-11-06

Queues in Tandem

  • Tandem: first hop \(M/M/1\), second is not
  • Long packet sees a drained second queue
  • Independence of service across hops is an assumption, not a fact

Time Reversibility and Burke

  • Time-reversible birth-death: departures Poisson at \(\lambda\)
  • Burke: occupancy independent of past departures
  • Independent service \(\Rightarrow\) tandem is two \(M/M/1\)

Kleinrock’s Independence Assumption

  • Each link \(\approx\) \(M/M/1\) after merging
  • \(\lambda_{ij}=\sum_s f_{ij}(s)x_s\)
  • \(\bar{N}_{ij}=\lambda_{ij}/(\mu_{ij}-\lambda_{ij})\)
  • Best when the network is dense and traffic is mixed

Jackson’s Theorem

  • Jackson: product form under Kleinrock + random routing
  • \(\bar{N}=\sum_{ij}\lambda_{ij}/(\mu_{ij}-\lambda_{ij})\)

A Five-Node Example

Five-node network used with Kleinrock’s assumption. Flow is from lower-numbered nodes to higher; thick routes carry the streams \(x_s\).
  • Worked five-node Kleinrock example
  • Most congested: \(1\to 2\) and \(3\to 5\)
  • Fastest \(1\to 5\): via node 4

Simulation
