Other Markov Queues

  • The \(M/M/1\) queue is perhaps the simplest Markov queue.
  • We will introduce two related Markov queues and compute their stationary distributions.

Other Markov Queues (continued)

  • A simple modification to the \(M/M/1\) queue: a finite length modification \(M/M/1/m\)
  • The infinite server queue, \(M/M/\infty\).

Detailed Balance

Flow rates at a boundary for \(M/M/\infty\) queue.

Detailed Balance (continued)

  • Make use of the standard detailed balance equations for this queue.
  • From the flow rates at the boundary we have in general\[ \lambda_{k-1}P\left(k-1\right)=\mu_{k}P\left(k\right)\]
  • There is infinite service capacity in the queue the service rate at state \(k\) is simply \(\mu_{k}=k\mu\).
  • The rate of arrivals stays constant, \(\lambda_{k}=\lambda\), so for this queue detailed balance gives.\[ \lambda P\left(k-1\right)=k\mu P\left(k\right)\]
  • Reorganising in terms of \(P\left(k\right)\) then gives\[ P\left(k\right)=\frac{\lambda}{k\mu}P\left(k-1\right)\]and therefore\[ P\left(k\right)=P\left(0\right)\left(\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\right)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}.\]

Normalisation Condition

  • Using the normalisation condition for this queue, \[ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P\left(k\right)=1,\]we obtain \(P\left(0\right)\) as P(0)=^{-1}.

The Offered Load

  • It is convenient to define the offered load, \[ a=\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\]as arrival rate divided by service rate.
  • This is a dimensionless constant, but has international standard units of Erlangs (after A.  K.  Erlang).

The Offered Load (continued)

  • Substituting this definition back into (\(\ref{eq:MMinftyP0}\)) we have, \[ P\left(0\right)=\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a^{k}}{k!}\right]^{-1}.\]
  • Recalling that \[ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\exp\left(x\right)\] we have\[ P\left(0\right)=\exp\left(-a\right)\]and the steady state distribution is then\[ P\left(k\right)=\frac{a^{k}}{k!}\exp\left(-a\right)\]
  • Which is recognised as a Poisson distribution with parameter \(a\).

Utilization Factor

  • Notice that the utilization factor for this system, \(\rho\), doesn’t really exist.
  • For the \(M/M/\infty\) there is infinite capacity so we can’t ask what percentage is in use!
  • The proporation of time that some part of the system is in use is\[ 1-P\left(0\right)=1-\exp\left(-a\right).\]
