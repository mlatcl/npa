Erlang Delay \(M\)/\(M\)/\(m\)

Queueing model of the Erlang delay system.

Erlang Delay \(M\)/\(M\)/\(m\) (continued)

  • We have considered the situation were there are as many servers as there are customers.
  • More realistic real world situations involve limited numbers of servers.
  • For example, call centres.
  • The system is known as the Erlang delay system. In Kendall’s notation it is written \(M/M/m\).
  • The `average holding time’, \(\tau=\frac{1}{\mu}\equiv\bar{X}\), which, for a network involving telephones is the time a call is open.

State Diagram

State diagram of an Erlang delay system.

State Diagram (continued)

  • The `death rate’ \(\mu_{k}\) has different definitions in different parts of the state space.
  • We need to consider detailed balance for each part in turn.

Detailed Balance

  • The Erlang delay system may be analysed as a birth-death process with birth rate \[ \lambda_{k}=\lambda\] and death rate \[ \mu_{k}=\left\{ {cc} k\mu & k=0,1,\dots,m-1\\ m\mu & k>m\right.\]}

The Two Situations

  • This means we have to consider two situations in detailed balance
  • When the system’s state, \(k\), is less than or equal to the number of servers.
  • When the system’s state, \(k\), is greater than the number of servers. We will cover this as an example.

Stationary Distribution

  • The overall stationary distribution is then\[ P\left(k\right)=\left\{ {cc} P\left(0\right)\frac{(m\rho)^{k}}{k!}, & k\leq m\\ P\left(0\right)\frac{m^{m}\rho^{k}}{m!} & k>m\right.\] \[ P\left(0\right)=\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{\left(m\rho\right)^{k}}{k!}+\frac{\left(m\rho\right)^{m}}{m!\left(1-\rho\right)}\right]^{-1}.\]Note that this relies on \(\rho<1\), if \(\lambda\geq m\mu\) the system has no stationary distribution.}

Probability of Waiting

  • For the \(M/M/m\) queue a key quantity of interest is the probability of of a customer queueing, \(P_{Q}\).
  • What is the probability, when the system is at steady state, that a customer will arrive and find all the \(m\) servers busy? P(km) & = & _{k=m}^{}P(k)\[ P\left(k\geq m\right)=P\left(0\right)\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\frac{m^{m}\rho^{k}}{m!}\]\[ P\left(k\geq m\right)=P\left(0\right)\frac{\left(\rho m\right)^{m}}{m!}\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\rho^{k-m},\]

Probability of Waiting contd

  • Substituting \(\dot{k}=k-m\) in the sum gives\[ P\left(k\geq m\right)=P\left(0\right)\frac{\left(\rho m\right)^{m}}{m!}\sum_{\dot{k}=1}^{\infty}\rho^{\dot{k}},\]
  • Making the usual substitution this can be re-written C(m,a)=P(km)=P(0).

Erlang C Formula

  • This is known as the Erlang C formula, or Erlang’s second formula after A.K. Erlang. By making use of our definition of \(a\) we can rewrite the offered load as\[ a=\frac{\lambda}{\mu}=m\rho\] and substituting into (\(\ref{eq:ErlangCone}\)) we have \[ C(m,a)=\frac{P\left(0\right)a^{m}}{m!\left(1-\frac{a}{m}\right)}.\]

Computing the Erlang C Formula

  • Can be computed on a computer: but
  • some care must be taken when computing it.

Computing the Erlang C Formula (continued)

  • when \(m\) is large overflow problems will occur for \(a^{m}\) and \(m!\).
  • Compute the formula in log space,\[ \ln C\left(m,a\right)=\ln P\left(0\right)+m\ln a-\ln m!-\ln\left(1-\frac{a}{m}\right).\]Still problems with \(\ln P\left(0\right)\) term, but each of the other terms can be computed with appropriate functions in Python (scipy.special.gammaln).
  • When we need to compute the Erlang C curve, for the purposes of exams and exercises, we will use the old fashioned method of curves.

Average number in System.

  • We can also compute the average number of calls in the Erlang Delay system, \[ \bar{N}=\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}C(m,a)+m\rho\]
  • These two terms represent the average queue length and the average number of busy lines.
  • The average queue length is given by \[ \bar{N}_{Q}=\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}C(m,a)\]

Average Queueing Time

  • Using Little’s formula the average waiting time is simply \[ \bar{W}=\frac{\bar{N}_{Q}}{\lambda}=\frac{C(m,a)}{m\mu(1-\rho)}.\]
  • Note that this is the average queueing time for all customers (whether they queued or not).

Queueing Time for those who Queue

  • Use the fact that the average queueing time for those who don’t queue is zero.
  • The average queueing time can be decomposed as\[ \bar{W}=C(m,a)\bar{W}_{Q}+\left(1-C(m,a)\right)\bar{W}_{\sim Q}\]

Queueing Time for those who Queue (continued)

  • \(\bar{W}_{Q}\) is the average queueing time for those who do queue
  • \(\bar{W}_{\sim Q}\) is the average queueing time for those who don’t queue.
  • Since \(\bar{W}_{\sim Q}=0\) the second term in this sum is zero we can rewrite in terms of \(\bar{W}_{Q}\) as \[ \bar{W_{Q}}=\frac{\bar{W}}{C(m,a)}=\frac{1}{m\mu(1-\rho)}=\frac{1}{\mu(m-a)}\]so the average queueing time for those who do queue is independent of the probability of queueing.
